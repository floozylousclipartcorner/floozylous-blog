Pressing Mistakes That Ruin Blanks

Every ruined blank has a story, and most of them start the same way: the press was too hot, the paper moved, or the blank went in damp. Five mistakes cause almost every pressing disaster — and every one of them is fixable before your next press.

The first two are about heat and water. Too much heat or too long scorches the blank and browns the edges; too little and the colours come out faded and washed out. Most blanks are happiest around 385–400°F for 45–90 seconds at medium pressure, but every press runs a little different — a test press on a spare blank beats a ruined good one. And always pre-press the blank for a few seconds first: trapped moisture is what causes ghosting and pale, patchy spots.

The next two are about movement and protection. If the paper shifts even a hair while the press is closed, the design ghosts — heat tape on the edges holds everything still. Fresh protective paper above and below the blank keeps stray ink off your press and stops scorch marks, and letting the design bleed a little past the edges keeps white lines off the finished piece.

The last one is patience. Let flat pieces cool flat under something heavy, or coasters curl as they cool. Five small habits, and the design on your screen is the design on the blank.
